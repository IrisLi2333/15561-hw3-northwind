00 Analytical framework

Diagnose,
prioritize, protect

A top-down management review that moves from the health of the whole business to the specific decisions it implies.

Why these three questions

These three questions follow the hierarchy of decisions Northwind's management faces. The first is the umbrella question: how strong is the business, and what is driving its growth? The second builds on that assessment and asks which markets and product categories deserve the nine-person team's limited sales effort. The third tests those priorities by examining what company-wide growth can conceal, such as a market losing sales or customers who place a first order and never return during the period observed.

Together, the questions help management decide how much confidence to place in current growth, where to invest next, and which weaknesses need attention to sustain that growth.

01 Overview: performance and growth quality

How is Northwind performing overall, what is driving its growth, and how broad-based and sustainable is that growth?

Why it matters

Management needs to understand both the scale and the quality of growth. Rising sales can reflect a larger customer base, more frequent purchases, larger orders, or unusually large purchases from a few accounts, and each pattern carries different implications for future performance. Concentration is the central test: a customer base weighted toward a few large accounts carries measurably higher risk, because the loss of one relationship removes a large share of revenue (Dhaliwal et al., 2016). Understanding the underlying drivers helps Northwind distinguish a broadly strengthening business from growth that depends heavily on a small number of customers, markets, or products.

02 Strategy: where to focus
02

Given a lean nine-person team, which markets and product categories should Northwind prioritize to sustain its growth?

Why it matters

A team of nine serving customers in 21 countries has limited capacity to pursue every opportunity. Northwind needs to focus its attention where there is credible evidence of meaningful sales potential and an achievable route to expansion. Prioritization should weigh existing business scale, incremental growth in dollars, and customer reach together with growth rates, since a small starting base can inflate a rate.

03 Risks: where growth is exposed
03

Beneath the headline growth, which products and markets are losing ground, and how well is Northwind retaining the customers it has won?

Why it matters

Aggregate growth can conceal deterioration in parts of the business. New customer purchases may offset weakening relationships with existing customers, while strong categories may mask declining products. Retention deserves particular weight: research on customer defection finds that small reductions in defection produce disproportionate gains in profit (Reichheld & Sasser, 1990). Identifying these patterns early helps management decide where to intervene, where to investigate changing demand, and where reduced attention may be appropriate.
